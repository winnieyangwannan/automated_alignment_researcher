### Isolation boundary explanation

Question: What is the isolation boundary, and how does it work?

Answer:

- The autonomous researcher and evaluator are separate trust domains. The researcher may inspect code, train models,
  submit checkpoints, and read only permitted feedback. The evaluator alone may read hidden test items, answers,
  rubrics, full held-out scores, and judge credentials.
- The research side writes a checkpoint plus submission marker to `SUBMISSIONS_DIR`. A separately running evaluator
  worker reads that checkpoint and `HOLDOUT_DIR`, scores it, writes research-safe aggregate feedback to `SCORES_DIR`,
  and writes the complete held-out result to `HELDOUT_SCORES_DIR`. The researcher polls only `SCORES_DIR`.
- `EVAL_VIA_WORKER=true` prevents the research process from running evaluation inline. The held-out suite and full
  held-out scores must be owned by a distinct evaluator Unix/service identity with inaccessible parent directories
  (normally mode `700`). Shared handoff directories need narrowly assigned cross-user permissions.
- This protects against adaptive overfitting and leakage: an autonomous agent with shell access could otherwise read
  hidden answers, optimize directly against the held-out metric, or inspect its full score after every attempt.
- Merely using different paths, `.env` files, containers, or mode `700` under the same Unix UID is not a security
  boundary. The same UID can read or change its own files. Without a second evaluator identity, the setup is only an
  honor-system smoke test and its held-out result is not paper-valid evidence of generalization.
- Before launch, the research identity should pass `scripts/verify_heldout_isolation.sh`: evaluator-private paths are
  unreadable, the held-out identifier is absent from research-readable artifacts, and `held_out_pct` is absent from
  the shared score channel.

Question: What is the easiest way around the missing separate evaluator identity on HPC?

Recommendation:

- For immediate setup validation, use a clearly labeled single-user smoke-test workflow and do not report its
  held-out/generalization result as paper-valid. This is fastest and sufficient to validate the environment, target
  model, training loop, submission handoff, and visible benchmark scoring.
- The smallest durable solution for valid automated experiments is to request one evaluator service/Unix account and
  one shared group. Keep the existing research account for AAR jobs; place holdout data, full held-out scores, and
  judge credentials under the evaluator account's mode-`700` storage; expose only per-team submissions and stripped
  scores through group-controlled shared directories. The repository already implements this two-user worker flow.
- A separate Slurm job, directory, virtual environment, container, or mode-`700` directory owned by the same UID is
  not an isolation boundary. The agent can still exercise that user's authority.
- If provisioning a second identity is temporarily impossible, a better procedural fallback is a one-shot final
  evaluation: keep evaluator-only materials absent during research, freeze the final checkpoint and commit, terminate
  all AAR processes, then have a human introduce and run the holdout once without feeding its result back into
  optimization. This is weaker than kernel-enforced isolation and requires a research checkout that does not expose
  evaluator-only benchmark material, but it avoids iterative held-out tuning.
- A remote evaluator service with its own credentials can substitute for a second local Unix user, but requires more
  infrastructure than the repository's existing two-user design.

### Splitting credentials across research and evaluator accounts

Question: If a new evaluator account is provisioned, how should evaluator and researcher credentials be configured?

Recommendation:

- Give each Unix identity its own mode-`600`, user-owned environment file. The research account may source
  `$HOME/.config/aar/research.env`; the evaluator account separately sources
  `$HOME/.config/aar/evaluator.env`. Never place evaluator secrets in the research worktree or research account.
- Research credentials: the Claude credential needed by the AAR, integrity monitor, and literature agents, plus a
  least-privilege Hugging Face token only if target-model/training access requires it. Do not provide the judge's
  `MODEL_API_KEY`, evaluator holdout paths, full held-out scores, or Petri judge credential.
- Evaluator credentials: `MODEL_API_KEY` for the configured benchmark judge, an evaluator-scoped Hugging Face token
  when gated evaluation data/models require it, and any separate Anthropic/Petri judge credential. It receives
  `HOLDOUT_DIR` and `HELDOUT_SCORES_DIR`, plus the same per-team `SUBMISSIONS_DIR` and `SCORES_DIR` used for handoff.
- Submit research Slurm jobs while logged in as the research user and evaluator workers while logged in as the
  evaluator user. Each job script sources only its own protected file. Do not put secret values in Git, `sbatch`
  command arguments, logs, or shared directories.
- The evaluator's home/private root and `.env` should be evaluator-owned and mode `700`/`600`. The two identities need
  a shared handoff group or narrowly scoped ACLs. For stronger integrity, research owns/writes submissions while
  evaluator has read access; evaluator owns/writes scores while research has read access.
- Do not leave evaluator SSH keys, forwarded credentials, passwordless `sudo`/`su`, or writable evaluator launch
  scripts available to the research account; any such path collapses the boundary.
- The current research `.env` contains `MODEL_API_KEY` for setup testing. Once the evaluator account is operational,
  copy that key securely into the evaluator's protected env, verify it there, and remove it from the research `.env`.
  Separate provider-issued keys per role are preferable when available because they can be revoked and audited
  independently.

### Full two-account AAR loop

Question: How does evaluator/researcher account setup work for a complete AAR loop?

Answer and deployment sequence:

1. Provision a research identity, a separate evaluator identity, and a narrowly scoped shared handoff group/ACL.
   The research identity must have no route to become the evaluator through SSH keys, forwarded agents, or sudo.
2. Deploy the same pinned repository commit and architecture-appropriate Python environment for both identities.
   Keep evaluator-only suites, benchmark materials, held-out outputs, and credentials outside the research checkout.
3. Research-private configuration holds the AAR/monitor/literature credential, target-model access if required,
   `AXIS`, `MODEL`, `HARNESS_TRANSPORT=fs`, and `EVAL_VIA_WORKER=true`. It has no judge key or eval-private paths.
4. Evaluator-private configuration holds `MODEL_API_KEY`, any gated-data/Petri credentials, `JUDGE_BACKEND`,
   `JUDGE_MODEL`, `HOLDOUT_DIR`, and `HELDOUT_SCORES_DIR`. Both roles receive the same per-team
   `SUBMISSIONS_DIR` and `SCORES_DIR` paths, but with directional ownership/ACLs.
5. Before any AAR starts, the evaluator publishes the holdout for the exact `(AXIS, MODEL)` into its mode-`700`
   storage. This also emits the held-out-stripped baseline table the research side is allowed to see.
6. The research launcher creates a unique `TEAM_ID`, shared team forum, method/log directories, and the handoff queue;
   literature agents populate the shared literature review, then multiple AAR chains begin proposing methods.
7. Each proposal passes the integrity monitor before a research-side GPU training job runs. The training job writes a
   checkpoint and submission marker under `SUBMISSIONS_DIR`, then polls `SCORES_DIR`.
8. One or more evaluator-owned workers watch that exact queue. A worker atomically claims each run, reads the submitted
   checkpoint plus private holdout, invokes the configured judges, writes only research-safe scores to `SCORES_DIR`,
   and writes complete held-out results to evaluator-private `HELDOUT_SCORES_DIR`.
9. The AAR reads permitted scores and shared findings, updates its method, and repeats. It never receives the holdout
   inputs, answers, complete held-out result, evaluator environment, or judge credential. A human reads final held-out
   results from the evaluator account after the run.
10. Before trusting a run, verify matching commit/axis/model/team paths, separate job owners, mode/ACLs, clean held-out
    leakage checks, and retained run metadata. Stop both sides on any mismatch.

that hierarchy does not exist on `fair-cw-use2-1`; the active worktree is under `/storage/home`. Before a full loop,
the launch scripts should be made configurable for repository, Python, runtime, shared handoff, and evaluator-private
roots, and each role should explicitly source its own protected env file.

### Meaning of kernel-enforced isolation

Question: What does “kernel-enforced” mean?

Answer: Linux associates every process with a numeric user/group identity and checks that identity against file
ownership, permission bits, and ACLs whenever the process tries to traverse a directory or open, read, write, rename,
or delete a file. If an AAR process running as the research UID attempts to read an evaluator-owned mode-`700`
holdout directory, the kernel rejects the system call with `EACCES` before any bytes are returned. Changing prompts,
Python code, shell commands, or environment variables cannot override that check. Slurm jobs retain the submitting
user's identity on compute nodes, while a shared filesystem must resolve the same UIDs/GIDs consistently.

This is stronger than telling an agent not to inspect a path, hiding the pathname, or using two `.env` files under
one account. It does not protect against root/cluster administrators, a kernel vulnerability, incorrect permissions,
or credentials that let the research account become the evaluator. If both processes use the same UID, mode `700`
does not isolate them because the kernel sees the same owner.

### Full autonomous two-account operating model

Question: How do the researcher and evaluator processes communicate automatically, and what actions start a full AAR
loop?

Answer:

- The two accounts do not SSH into each other and no human switches accounts per iteration. They communicate through
  a shared-filesystem queue. Research copies a completed model to `SUBMISSIONS_DIR/<run_id>/model` and atomically
  creates `SUBMISSIONS_DIR/<run_id>/.submitted`. An evaluator-owned watcher scans the queue, claims each run with an
  atomic claim directory, and launches an evaluation job. The evaluator writes stripped feedback to
  `SCORES_DIR/<run_id>.json`; that file is both the response and completion signal. Research polls for it, records the
  result, and begins the next proposal. Full held-out output is written only to evaluator-private storage.
- Operator startup is only three phases: evaluator publishes the `(axis, model)` holdout; researcher creates/launches
  a team and obtains its `TEAM_ID` plus shared queue paths; evaluator starts a long-running watcher for those exact
  paths. Slurm jobs persist after SSH logout. Once both sides are running, proposal, integrity review, GPU training,
  submission, evaluation, filtered feedback, finding sharing, and the next iteration proceed automatically.
- Prefer `launch_eval_watcher.sh` over the persistent GPU worker: it is a zero-GPU dispatcher that notices submission
  markers and launches short GPU evaluation jobs, while atomically preventing duplicate scoring and retrying jobs that
  die without producing a score. The research chains are likewise GPU-less while reasoning and submit separate GPU
  training jobs per approved method.
- Operational order: provision accounts/group/storage; deploy the same pinned commit and separate environments; split
  role credentials; create evaluator-private and shared per-team directories with verified ACLs; publish holdout as
  evaluator; launch a five-seed research team; capture `TEAM_ID`; launch evaluator watcher using that team's exact

### ACLs

Question: What are ACLs?

Answer: Filesystem Access Control Lists extend the normal owner/group/other permission bits by allowing rules for
specific named users and groups. For example, a submissions directory can grant the research user write access and
the evaluator read access, while a scores directory can reverse those roles. A default ACL makes new child files and
directories inherit the intended access. `getfacl <path>` displays the rules and `setfacl -m`/`setfacl -d -m` changes
access/default rules. The filesystem must support ACLs and resolve the same UIDs/GIDs on every cluster node.

ACLs do not create accounts, protect two processes using the same UID, or compensate for sudo/evaluator credentials
available to the research account. Also, the current repository writes research proposal metadata beneath
`SCORES_DIR/.proposals`; therefore making all of `SCORES_DIR` read-only to research would break the current code.
Either retain the repository-compatible shared-group write model or first move proposal metadata to a separate
research-writable directory before enforcing directional score ACLs.

### Librarian agents in the full AAR loop

Question: How do librarian agents work, and do they need a separate account like research and evaluation?

Answer:

- Librarians belong to the research trust domain and do not need a third OS account. They should run as the research
  user, with no evaluator credential, `HOLDOUT_DIR`, `HELDOUT_SCORES_DIR`, or private benchmark material.
- `launch_team.sh` creates the team directories and checks an axis-wide literature directory. If fewer than the target
  entries exist (default 30), it submits `scripts/litreview.sh` with `sbatch --wait`, so team launch blocks until the
  literature pre-phase finishes. An existing axis survey is reused unless refresh is requested; it can also be
  explicitly skipped.
- `aar.litreview.run_litreview` defines four sub-areas—general, axis-specific, mechanism, and recent/adjacent—and runs
  one Claude Sonnet 4.6 `BaseAgent` for each. Each agent can use WebSearch, WebFetch, Read, Write, `get_literature`, and
  `share_literature`, and writes structured paper/method reproduction guides. Despite the paper's parallel-agent
  description, the current implementation iterates over these four sub-areas sequentially to control API/web rate.
- The pre-run survey is stored in `LIT_AXIS_DIR` as a reusable, axis-wide baseline. During the experiment, each team
  writes new literature discoveries to its own `TEAM_DIR/litreview` via `LIT_FORUM_DIR`. `get_literature` returns the
  union of the read-only axis baseline and that team's additions, deduplicated. Every AAR chain must consult it at the
  start of each iteration and may append useful public research it finds.
- Required setup is research-side only: `LITREVIEW_MODEL=claude-sonnet-4-6`, an Anthropic-compatible credential for
  the Claude agent path, outbound WebSearch/WebFetch capability, a small CPU Slurm allocation, and writable research
  literature/workspace paths. A distinct librarian API key is optional for budgets/auditing, not isolation.
- Keep the librarian workspace and outputs research-readable but evaluator-secret-free. It may survey public papers
  and benchmark methodology, but must not receive hidden items, answers, full held-out scores, judge credentials, or
  evaluator filesystem access. The integrity monitor remains responsible for rejecting proposed training methods
  that use prohibited evaluation data or stronger-model distillation.
- The current `scripts/litreview.sh` still fails shell syntax validation and contains original `/opt/aar/...`
  deployment paths, so it must be repaired/ported before either the single-user smoke test or a two-account run.

### Research-side launch sequence

Question: Are the librarian search and AAR loop launched separately, or does one launcher run them in sequence?

Answer: The intended operator interface is one research-side command, `scripts/launch_team.sh`. Internally it is a
two-phase sequence. First, the launcher checks `LIT_AXIS_DIR`; if the reusable axis survey has fewer than
`LITREVIEW_MIN` entries (default 30), it submits `scripts/litreview.sh` as a separate CPU Slurm job using
`sbatch --wait` and blocks until that job exits. If enough entries already exist, it reuses them; `LITREVIEW_SKIP=1`
can explicitly skip the phase and `LITREVIEW_REFRESH=1` can rebuild it. Second, after the literature baseline is
ready, the same launcher submits the AAR chain jobs. Thus the jobs are separate at the scheduler level but sequenced
automatically by one launcher; the user normally does not invoke the librarian manually.

The evaluator watcher remains a separately submitted long-running job, because it belongs to the evaluator trust
domain. It may start as soon as the holdout is published and the research launcher has printed/created the team's
`TEAM_ID`, `SUBMISSIONS_DIR`, and `SCORES_DIR`. Once librarian, AAR chains, and watcher are submitted, all later
training/evaluation iterations communicate through the queue automatically without operator relaunches.
